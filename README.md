## What "Unmetered" Actually Means (and What It Doesn't)

Here's the part most comparison articles gloss over. Unmetered is not the same as unlimited. A 1Gbps unmetered port can theoretically push about 330TB in a month if it runs flat-out, every second, no sleep. A 10Gbps unmetered port? Multiply that by ten. The provider isn't counting your bytes — they're capping your lane width. Push the lane as hard as you want; just don't expect to exceed the lane.

This matters because it changes the question you should be asking. Instead of "how much bandwidth do I get," the better question is "what port speed do I need, and is the port genuinely dedicated to me or shared with a hundred other tenants?" A real unmetered dedicated server gives you a port that's yours, on hardware that's yours, with no noisy neighbors stealing your throughput at peak hours.

That's the whole point of going dedicated in the first place. You're not buying bandwidth in the abstract — you're buying a predictable, isolated slice of physical infrastructure.

## Who Actually Needs an Unmetered Dedicated Server

Not everyone does, and pretending otherwise is how people end up overpaying. If you're running a brochure site that gets 400 visits a day, a $5 VPS laughs at your needs. Unmetered dedicated hardware earns its keep when traffic is heavy, sustained, or bursty in ways that would torch a metered plan:

- **Streaming and media delivery** — video, audio, large file distribution where every gigabyte counts against you on a metered plan.
- **Game servers and high-traffic communities** — the kind of workloads where a single popular match can spike bandwidth hard, and where DDoS attacks are a near-daily reality rather than a theoretical risk.
- **Backup, replication, and storage nodes** — moving terabytes between data centers on a schedule, where overage fees would make the math ugly fast.
- **SaaS and API backends** with growing user bases and unpredictable spikes.
- **VPN and proxy infrastructure** where constant tunneled traffic is the entire product.

If any of those sound like your Tuesday, an unmetered dedicated server stops being a luxury and starts being the only plan that doesn't punish you for success.

## Bare Metal vs. Cloud: A Quick Reality Check

If you're still waffling between a bare-metal unmetered box and a cloud instance, the decision really comes down to one question: do you need predictable, sustained, high-CPU/RAM/disk-IO performance, or do you need elastic scaling?

**Pick bare metal unmetered when** your workload is resource-heavy and steady — game servers, streaming nodes, always-on API backends, storage clusters. You get the full machine, no hypervisor overhead, no noisy neighbors, and a flat bandwidth bill you can model in a spreadsheet.

**Pick cloud when** your traffic is spiky and unpredictable, you want hourly billing, or you'd rather not think about hardware at all. Cloud gives you redundancy and scale; bare metal gives you raw performance and cost certainty.
